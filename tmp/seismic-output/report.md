# Global Seismic Activity Report: 2025

*Prepared from USGS FDSN event data, NOAA NCEI tsunami records, USGS PAGER alerts, and open-source incident reporting.*

---

## Executive Summary

The year 2025 was defined by a stark contrast between seismic energy and human impact. A total of 144 M6.0+ earthquakes were recorded globally — distributed sharply across regions, with Asia-Pacific accounting for 94 events (65%), the Americas for 39, and Europe-Africa for just 11. The year's most powerful event, an M8.8 megathrust on the Kamchatka Peninsula on July 29, was the largest earthquake anywhere on Earth since the 2011 Tōhoku disaster, yet it killed fewer than 5 people. Five months earlier, the M7.7 Myanmar earthquake — less than one percent of Kamchatka's energy release — killed up to 5,352 people, injured more than 11,000, and displaced roughly 17 million. This asymmetry — between magnitude and mortality, between remote energy release and urban catastrophe — is the defining pattern of global seismicity in 2025.

Across all three regions, the year demonstrated consistent underperformance of tsunamis relative to warning forecasts. Major tsunami warnings were issued following large events in the Pacific, Atlantic, Arctic, and Aegean, yet no tsunami directly caused a single fatality. Evacuation systems performed well; wave heights were consistently below forecast maximums. The Americas saw an unprecedented clustering of large-magnitude events in the Drake Passage — three M7.4+ earthquakes in a single calendar year from one of the most remote seismic zones on Earth. Meanwhile, Europe-Africa remained the quietest major region, with no M7.0+ events and all significant human impacts concentrated in Turkey's persistently vulnerable building stock. Globally, 2025 reinforced that seismic risk is driven far more by population exposure, depth, and construction quality than by earthquake magnitude alone.

Total confirmed deaths attributable to 2025 seismic events: approximately 3,715–5,462, with the Myanmar M7.7 accounting for over 95% of global earthquake fatalities. Kamchatka's M8.8 — by far the year's largest energy release — caused a single indirect death.

## Americas

### Regional Overview

The Americas experienced 39 M6.0+ earthquakes in 2025, ranging from M6.0 to M7.6. Seismically active zones included the Alaska-Aleutian arc, the Caribbean plate boundary, the Drake Passage/Scotia arc, and northern South America. The headline story of the region was a remarkable concentration of major activity in the Drake Passage, a remote ocean corridor at 60°S where three M7.4+ events struck within a single year.

### Major Events

| Date | Magnitude | Location | Depth | Impact Summary |
|------|-----------|----------|-------|----------------|
| 2025-02-08 | M7.6 | Cayman Islands (SW Caribbean) | 14.3 km | Tsunami warning issued and canceled; no casualties or damage |
| 2025-05-02 | M7.4 | Drake Passage (~222 km S of Ushuaia) | 10 km | ~1,800 evacuated in Chile; no waves materialized |
| 2025-07-16 | M7.3 | Sand Point, Alaska Peninsula | 38 km | Tsunami warning; evacuations; 3-inch wave only; 2,700+ aftershocks |
| 2025-08-22 | M7.5 | Southern Drake Passage (~710 km SE of Ushuaia) | 10 km | Antarctic coast advisory only; no evacuation orders |
| 2025-09-24/25 | M6.2/M6.3 | Zulia State, Venezuela (Mene Grande) | 9–14 km | 1 death, ~110 injuries, 44+ homes collapsed |
| 2025-10-10 | M7.6 | Drake Passage (~60°S) | 5.6 km | Tsunami warning issued and canceled within ~1 hour; no impact |

### Impact

Deaths in the Americas totaled just one — a cardiac event attributed to panic during the Venezuela M6.2/M6.3 sequence — despite four separate M7+ earthquake/tsunami warning pairs. The Venezuela sequence also produced approximately 110 injuries and 44+ building collapses in Zulia State, an oil-producing region near the South Caribbean Deformed Belt. Every M7+ event in the hemisphere occurred in remote ocean settings; all tsunami warnings were canceled within approximately one hour; maximum confirmed wave height was three inches.

The Cayman Islands M7.6 on February 8 was the most powerful Caribbean earthquake since the Haiti M7.2 of 2021, occurring on the Swan Islands transform fault — a left-lateral strike-slip structure at the North American/Caribbean plate boundary. The Sand Point M7.3 in Alaska generated one of the year's most productive aftershock sequences: 40+ aftershocks in the first three hours and 2,700+ total through the end of July.

### Geological Context

The Americas' seismic distribution in 2025 reflects four distinct tectonic settings: the Alaska-Aleutian subduction zone, the North American/Caribbean transform boundary, the remote Scotia Arc and Drake Passage, and the northern South American margin. The Drake Passage's three M7.4+ events all occurred at shallow depths (5.6–10 km) on the interaction boundary of the South American, Antarctic, and Scotia plates — a zone of complex, shallow crustal seismicity extremely distant from populated coastlines. Venezuela's sequence struck the Boconó Fault system within the South Caribbean Deformed Belt, a historically destructive zone adjacent to densely populated areas and oil infrastructure.

### Notable Patterns

**Drake Passage Clustering.** Three M7.4+ events struck the same remote ocean zone in May, August, and October — an extraordinary annual concentration. All triggered tsunami warnings or advisories; none produced damaging waves.

**Tsunami Warning Performance.** All four tsunami warnings issued for Americas events in 2025 were canceled within approximately one hour. Warning systems triggered appropriate precautionary evacuations; event geometry and depth limited actual wave generation in each case.

**Magnitude vs. Human Impact Inversion.** The only event with casualties was M6.2/M6.3 Venezuela — well below the year's top-magnitude threshold. All M7+ events occurred at sea. Local construction quality and population proximity dominated impact outcomes over raw magnitude.

**Alaska Aftershock Productivity.** The Sand Point M7.3 reflects the well-documented seismic productivity of the Alaska-Aleutian subduction zone: 2,700+ aftershocks generated by a single M7.3 event, with the largest aftershock (M6.2, July 20) occurring four days after the mainshock.

## Asia-Pacific

### Regional Overview

Asia-Pacific was the dominant seismic region of 2025 by every measure: 94 of 144 global M6.0+ events (65%), the year's four largest earthquakes, and more than 95% of global seismic fatalities. The region spans a wide geographic arc — from Russia's Kamchatka Peninsula through Japan, the Philippines, and down to Myanmar — with nearly all of its most significant events clustered along Ring of Fire subduction zones and one major continental transform fault.

### Major Events

| Date | Magnitude | Location | Key Impact |
|------|-----------|----------|------------|
| 2025-03-28 | M7.7 | Mandalay, Myanmar | Up to 5,352 killed; 200,000 displaced; deadliest event of 2025 |
| 2025-07-29 | M8.8 | Kamchatka Peninsula, Russia | Largest earthquake globally since 2011; Pacific-wide tsunami; 1.9M evacuated in Japan |
| 2025-09-18 | M7.8 | E of Petropavlovsk-Kamchatsky | Largest Kamchatka aftershock; tsunami warning; no significant impact |
| 2025-10-10 | M7.4 | Santiago, Philippines (Mindanao) | At least 6 killed; hospital and school damage; small tsunami |
| 2025-12-08 | M7.6 | Aomori Prefecture, Japan | 47 injured; 90,000+ evacuated; 70 cm tsunami observed |

### Impact

The Myanmar M7.7 of March 28 was the deadliest earthquake of 2025 and one of the most lethal in Asia in years. Centered near Mandalay at a depth of only 10 km, the event killed between 3,600 and 5,352 people, injured more than 11,000, and affected an estimated 17 million. More than 157,000 buildings were damaged or destroyed, including three hospitals and the historic Ava Bridge (constructed 1934). In neighboring Thailand, collapsing structures in Bangkok — triggered by the distant shaking — killed 103 additional people.

The M8.8 Kamchatka event of July 29 was the year's most energetic earthquake by a large margin, and the strongest globally since the 2011 Tōhoku M9.0. Despite its extraordinary magnitude, it caused only one indirect fatality and 21 injuries during evacuation — a result of its extremely remote location and Japan's world-class tsunami warning infrastructure. Nearly 1.9 million people evacuated along Japan's Pacific coast following the warning. Observed tsunami heights were notably lower than forecast maximums, attracting scientific interest regarding source heterogeneity and rupture geometry.

The Aomori M7.6 (December 8) struck closer to population centers, injuring 47 and prompting evacuation of 90,000+ in northeastern Japan. A 70 cm tsunami was observed. Approximately 4,000 buildings were damaged.

### Geological Context

Four of Asia-Pacific's five major events occurred on Ring of Fire subduction structures. The Kamchatka megathrust lies on the Kuril-Kamchatka Trench, where the Pacific Plate subducts beneath the Okhotsk microplate at 79–85 mm/year — a zone with historical precedent for extreme events (1952 M8.8–9.0, 1737 ~M9.0–9.3). The Aomori event struck the Japan Trench, the same subduction zone as the 2011 Tōhoku disaster. The Philippines event occurred at the Philippine Trench/Mindanao segment, one of the world's most seismically complex boundaries.

The Myanmar M7.7 was the critical exception: a continental strike-slip event on the Sagaing Fault, a 1,400 km right-lateral transform separating the Burma Microplate from the Sunda Plate. The rupture broke a seismic gap that had been accumulating stress since 1839 and was classified as a supershear event — rupture velocity exceeded local shear wave speed, concentrating destructive energy forward along the fault strike and amplifying damage in Mandalay and the Sagaing region.

### Notable Patterns

**Kamchatka Sequence Clustering.** An M7.4 foreshock on July 20 preceded the M8.8 mainshock by nine days. The main sequence generated 2,877+ aftershocks, including two major events (M7.4 September 13, M7.8 September 18) more than 45 days post-mainshock — illustrating extended seismic hazard windows following megathrust ruptures.

**Supershear Rupture (Myanmar).** The Sagaing Fault ruptured along a 530 km section at supershear velocity — a rare mode that amplifies forward-directed ground motion. The combination of shallow depth, urban density, vulnerable building stock, and supershear dynamics made this the year's most lethal event despite being only its third-largest by magnitude.

**Tsunami Underperformance.** Four of the five major Asia-Pacific events with tsunami warnings produced wave heights well below forecast maximums. Zero tsunami-direct fatalities were recorded across the entire region.

**Human Impact Asymmetry.** The M8.8 Kamchatka event (year's largest energy release) caused approximately 1 death. The M7.7 Myanmar event (third-largest magnitude) caused up to 5,352 deaths. Depth, urbanization, construction quality, and rupture mode — not magnitude — determined human outcomes.

## Europe-Africa

### Regional Overview

Europe-Africa was the quietest of the three major regions in 2025, recording just 11 M6.0+ earthquakes with a maximum magnitude of M6.6. No M7.0+ events occurred. Despite the relatively subdued activity, the region recorded two fatalities — both in Turkey — and significant building damage in Turkish and Greek coastal zones. The geographic spread ran from the Arctic (Jan Mayen, 71°N) through the Mediterranean to the South Atlantic and South Sandwich Islands.

### Major Events

| Date | Magnitude | Location | Depth | Impact Summary |
|------|-----------|----------|-------|----------------|
| 2025-01-01 | M6.1 | South Sandwich Islands | 70 km | Remote; no impact |
| 2025-03-10 | M6.5 | Jan Mayen, Arctic Norway | 10 km | Tsunami bulletin issued; downgraded; <20 residents |
| 2025-03-28 | M6.6 | Central Mid-Atlantic Ridge | 12.3 km | Largest event in region; remote; zero impact |
| 2025-03-28 | M6.1 | Central Mid-Atlantic Ridge | 10 km | Likely co-seismic pair with M6.6 on same transform segment |
| 2025-04-23 | M6.2 | Marmara Ereğlisi, Turkey | 12.7 km | 1 fatality, 359+ injured, ~4,295 buildings damaged |
| 2025-05-13 | M6.0 | Fry, Greece (Dodecanese) | 76 km | Deep intraslab; widely felt; no damage |
| 2025-05-22 | M6.2 | Karpathos Sea, Greece | 64 km | Buildings damaged (Crete); 5 cm tsunami at Heraklion |
| 2025-05-30 | M6.2 | SW of Africa | 10 km | Remote; no impact |
| 2025-06-25 | M6.2 | Southern Mid-Atlantic Ridge | 10 km | Remote; no impact |
| 2025-08-10 | M6.1 | Bigadiç, Turkey | 10 km | 1 fatality, 52 injured, 863+ buildings damaged |
| 2025-10-27 | M6.0 | Sındırgı, Turkey | 8 km | Shallow; no confirmed casualties |

### Impact

The region's two fatalities and the majority of its injuries and structural damage were produced by Turkey's three events. The Marmara Ereğlisi M6.2 on April 23 was the region's most impactful event: 1 fatality, 359+ injuries, and approximately 4,295 buildings damaged across the Marmara region at a maximum felt intensity of MMI VIII. The Bigadiç M6.1 on August 10 added a second fatality and 52 injuries, with 863+ buildings damaged in Balıkesir Province. A third Turkish event (Sındırgı M6.0, October 27) struck at only 8 km depth but produced no confirmed casualties.

Greece's two events were both intermediate-depth intraslab events (64–76 km) that were widely felt but caused limited damage. The Karpathos Sea M6.2 of May 22 produced a 5 cm tsunami at Heraklion, Crete — consistent with Hellenic Arc geometry — but no coastal impact. The Jan Mayen M6.5 (March 10) prompted a precautionary tsunami bulletin for Arctic Norway's tiny Jan Mayen Island (population <20); the bulletin was quickly downgraded.

### Geological Context

Turkey's seismic activity reflects two distinct fault systems: the North Anatolian Fault (NAF), a right-lateral strike-slip boundary crossing northern Turkey, produced the Marmara Ereğlisi event. This portion of the NAF lies approximately 65 km west of Istanbul; the segment last ruptured catastrophically in 1766 and carries an estimated 35–70% probability of an M7+ event in coming decades. The Bigadiç and Sındırgı events occurred in the Aegean Extensional Province — a zone of shallow normal faulting driven by back-arc spreading behind the Hellenic subduction zone.

Greece's intermediate-depth events originate from the Hellenic Arc, where the African plate subducts northward beneath the Aegean microplate at approximately 35 mm/year — the most seismically active zone in Europe. The Mid-Atlantic Ridge events (both occurring March 28 on the same equatorial transform segment) and the southern ocean events represent routine plate-boundary activity with no hazard consequence.

### Notable Patterns

**Turkey: Disproportionate Human Impact.** Turkey produced 3 of 11 regional events (27%) but 100% of fatalities and the vast majority of injuries. Two distinct tectonic regimes are implicated: NAF strike-slip (Marmara) and Aegean extensional normal faulting (Balıkesir/Sındırgı). The Marmara event is notable as a stress signal on the Istanbul segment of the NAF.

**Mediterranean Arc: Deep Events, Wide Felt Radii.** Both Greek events were intermediate-depth intraslab events (64–76 km), producing lower near-field intensities but felt across the entire eastern Mediterranean basin. Both generated measurable but non-damaging tsunamis.

**Magnitude-Impact Inversion.** The region's largest event (M6.6, Mid-Atlantic Ridge) caused zero human impact. The most impactful events were M6.2 and M6.1 — due to shallow depth, proximity to population, and vulnerable building stock in western Turkey.

**Tsunami Activity: Three Events, Zero Coastal Impact.** Three events triggered tsunami notifications (Jan Mayen, Marmara, Karpathos). Maximum observed wave heights: 6 cm at Erdek (Sea of Marmara), 5 cm at Heraklion. The enclosed geometry of the Marmara Sea and the moderate magnitude of all triggering events kept tsunami hazard subdued.

## Global Trends and Cross-Regional Analysis

### Asia-Pacific Dominance

Asia-Pacific produced 65% of global M6.0+ events and every event above M7.0 except the Cayman Islands M7.6 and the Alaska M7.3. The region's seismic output reflects its position on the world's most active subduction arc system. The Americas contributed 27% of global events; Europe-Africa just 8%. This distribution is broadly consistent with long-term historical baselines.

### The Magnitude-Mortality Disconnect

The most consistent global pattern of 2025 was the near-total decoupling of earthquake magnitude from human impact. This held across all three regions:

| Event | Magnitude | Deaths |
|-------|-----------|--------|
| Kamchatka, Russia | M8.8 | ~1 |
| Myanmar | M7.7 | up to 5,352 |
| Cayman Islands | M7.6 | 0 |
| Aomori, Japan | M7.6 | 0 (47 injured) |
| Drake Passage (×3) | M7.4–7.6 | 0 |
| Alaska | M7.3 | 0 |
| Marmara, Turkey | M6.2 | 1 |
| Venezuela | M6.2 | 1 |
| Bigadiç, Turkey | M6.1 | 1 |

Three M6.2 events — in Turkey (×2) and Venezuela — account for the majority of global earthquake fatalities outside Myanmar. The determinants were consistent: shallow depth (<15 km), proximity to populated areas, and construction vulnerability. Remoteness and depth were the dominant protective factors in every high-magnitude low-mortality event.

### Tsunami Warning System Performance

Globally, at least seven major tsunami warnings or advisories were issued in 2025 (Americas: 4; Asia-Pacific: 3+; Europe-Africa: 2 minor). Zero people died directly from a tsunami. Wave heights consistently fell below forecast maximums, most notably in the Kamchatka M8.8 sequence, where the Pacific-wide warning produced large-scale evacuation of 1.9 million people in Japan but waves well below predictions. Precautionary evacuations functioned well in Alaska, Chile, and northeastern Japan. This pattern represents a global success for early-warning infrastructure.

### Subduction Zone vs. Continental Fault Asymmetry

Four of the five largest earthquakes globally were subduction zone events. Yet the deadliest by far was the exception: the Myanmar M7.7, which originated on a continental strike-slip fault. Subduction-related megathrust events (Kamchatka, Aomori) and oceanic transform events (Cayman Islands, Drake Passage) occurred in settings that buffered their human impact through distance, depth, or infrastructure. Myanmar's Sagaing Fault rupture combined a rare tectonic setting (supershear strike-slip on a long-locked gap), shallow focus, and dense urban exposure in a way no subduction event in 2025 replicated.

### Seismic Clustering: Drake Passage and Kamchatka

Two notable clustering sequences stand out:

- **Drake Passage (Americas):** Three M7.4+ events in May, August, and October at the same remote subduction arc. All three were very shallow (5.6–10 km) with high theoretical tsunami-generation potential; all three produced no damaging waves due to extreme distance from coastlines.

- **Kamchatka (Asia-Pacific):** A foreshock–mainshock–aftershock sequence spanning at least five months, featuring an M7.4 foreshock (July 20), an M8.8 mainshock (July 29), and major aftershocks including an M7.8 (September 18) — demonstrating that megathrust seismic hazard windows can remain elevated for many months after the primary event.

### Comparison to Historical Norms

The M8.8 Kamchatka event is a once-per-decade-class earthquake; no event of comparable magnitude had been recorded globally since Tōhoku 2011. The year's overall count of 144 M6.0+ events is broadly consistent with long-term averages (approximately 130–150 per year globally). The Drake Passage produced an unusually high concentration of M7.4+ events for a single remote subregion. Myanmar's M7.7 was the most destructive earthquake in Southeast Asia since the 2015 Nepal M7.8, and the supershear classification places it among a small number of well-documented supershear events globally.
