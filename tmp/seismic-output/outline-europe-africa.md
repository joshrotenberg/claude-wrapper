# Europe-Africa Region: Outline for Global Seismic Activity Report (2025)

---

## 1. Regional Overview

- **Event count:** 11 M6.0+ earthquakes (fewest of the three global regions; cf. 94 Asia-Pacific, 39 Americas)
- **Magnitude range:** M6.0–M6.6
- **No M7.0+ events recorded in this region in 2025**
- **Geographic spread:**
  - North Atlantic / Arctic: Jan Mayen (71°N), central Mid-Atlantic Ridge (equatorial)
  - Mediterranean / Aegean: western Turkey, Karpathos Sea (Greece), Dodecanese (Greece)
  - South Atlantic: southern Mid-Atlantic Ridge, South Sandwich Islands, southwest of Africa
- **Split by setting:**
  - Continental / near-population: 6 events (Turkey ×3, Greece ×2, South Sandwich Islands ×1)
  - Remote ocean: 5 events (Mid-Atlantic Ridge ×3, SW Africa ×1, South Sandwich Islands treated separately above due to depth)

## 3. Impact Analysis

### Casualties
- **Total fatalities:** 2 (both in Turkey)
  - Marmara Ereğlisi (Apr 23): 1 (cardiac arrest attributed to earthquake panic)
  - Bigadiç (Aug 10): 1
- **Total injured:** 411+ (359+ Marmara, 52 Bigadiç)
- All other events: zero casualties

### Building Damage
- **Marmara Ereğlisi:** ~4,295 buildings damaged across the Marmara region (MMI VIII max)
- **Bigadiç:** 863+ buildings damaged in Balıkesir Province; 6 destroyed; 8+ homes and 105 buildings in Manisa Province; sinkhole reported
- **Karpathos/Crete:** Several buildings damaged in Heraklion area; landslides near Marathitis
- **Sındırgı:** Shallow depth (8 km) suggests potential for damage; details unavailable

### Economic Damage
- No formal economic loss estimates available from PAGER for these events
- Two Orange PAGER alerts (Marmara, Bigadiç) indicate significant but sub-catastrophic exposure
- Turkey's building stock in the Aegean and Marmara zones faces persistent vulnerability to moderate-shallow events

### Tsunami Impact
- Three events triggered tsunami notifications; none caused coastal damage (see Section 5)

## 4. Geological Context

### Mediterranean Seismic Systems
- **North Anatolian Fault (NAF):** Right-lateral strike-slip system crossing northern Turkey; the locked Marmara segment (~65 km west of Istanbul) is the primary source of the Apr 23 M6.2; last major rupture on this segment was 1766; estimated 35–70% probability of M7+ in coming decades
- **Aegean Extensional Province:** E-W normal faulting driven by back-arc spreading behind the Hellenic subduction zone; source of Bigadiç (Aug) and Sındırgı (Oct) events; characterized by shallow (<15 km), brittle-crust normal faults
- **Hellenic Arc / African subduction:** African plate subducting northward beneath the Aegean microplate at ~35 mm/year; source of both Greek events (M6.2 Karpathos, M6.0 Fry); intraslab events at 64–76 km depth; most seismically active zone in Europe

### Atlantic Ocean Ridge Systems
- **Central Mid-Atlantic Ridge (equatorial):** Paired M6.6 + M6.1 events on the same east-west transform fault segment (Mar 28); routine strike-slip kinematics; zero hazard
- **Southern Mid-Atlantic Ridge:** Single M6.2 event; normal plate-boundary spreading activity
- **Jan Mayen Fracture Zone / Kolbeinsey Ridge:** Complex triple-junction (Jan Mayen Ridge microcontinent + Kolbeinsey spreading segment + Jan Mayen Fracture Zone); oblique strike-slip with thrust component; among the most significant events recorded at Jan Mayen in recent decades

### Remote Southern Ocean
- **South Sandwich Islands (Jan 1):** M6.1 at 70 km depth; subduction-related intraslab event; no impact

## 5. Notable Patterns

### Turkey: Disproportionate Human Impact
- Turkey produced 3 of 11 events (27%) but 100% of fatalities and the vast majority of injuries
- Two distinct tectonic regimes involved: NAF strike-slip (Marmara) and Aegean extensional normal faulting (Balıkesir/Sındırgı)
- Sındırgı M6.0 (Oct 27) at 8 km depth is shallower than Bigadiç — represents continued seismic loading in the same extensional zone
- The Marmara event, while moderate, is significant as a stress-loading signal on the Istanbul segment of the NAF

### Mediterranean Arc: Deep Events, Wide Felt Radii
- Greece's two events were both intermediate-depth intraslab events (64 and 76 km)
- Despite lower near-field intensities, deep Hellenic Arc events are felt across the entire eastern Mediterranean basin
- Both generated small but measurable tsunamis consistent with Aegean/eastern Mediterranean bathymetry

### Mid-Atlantic Ridge Co-Seismic Pair (March 28)
- M6.6 and M6.1 struck the same equatorial transform segment within hours
- Coordinates (0.756°N/0.706°N, ~29.7°W) indicate same fault system
- Likely foreshock-mainshock or mainshock-aftershock relationship; no hazard consequence

### Tsunami Activity: Three Events, Zero Coastal Impact
| Event | Wave Height | Location | Notes |
|-------|-------------|----------|-------|
| Jan Mayen M6.5 (Mar 10) | Not recorded | N/A | Precautionary bulletin; downgraded; <20 residents |
| Marmara Ereğlisi M6.2 (Apr 23) | 6 cm at Erdek | Sea of Marmara | Enclosed basin limits propagation |
| Karpathos M6.2 (May 22) | 5 cm at Heraklion | Eastern Mediterranean | Hellenic Arc subduction geometry |

- Absence of a major Hellenic Arc thrust event (M7+) kept regional tsunami risk subdued in 2025

### Magnitude-Impact Inversion
- The largest event (M6.6, Mid-Atlantic Ridge) caused zero human impact
- The most impactful events were M6.2 and M6.1 — significantly smaller — due to shallow depth, proximity to population, and vulnerable building stock
- This inversion is the defining characteristic of the Europe-Africa dataset in 2025
