# Americas Significant Earthquakes 2025 — Research Report

**Source data:** USGS FDSN Event API, 2025 M6.0+ events
**Region total:** 39 events (M6.0–7.6)
**Research date:** 2026-03-19

---

## Overview

The Americas recorded 39 M6.0+ earthquakes in 2025. The top-magnitude events were dominated by a remarkable cluster in the **Drake Passage** (three M7.4+ events in a single year), alongside a significant Caribbean event near the Cayman Islands and a notable Alaskan event. The only event with confirmed human casualties was a lower-magnitude (M6.2/M6.3) sequence in Venezuela.

---

## Top 5 Events by Magnitude

### 1. M7.6 — Cayman Islands (2025-02-08)

**Location:** 210 km SSW of George Town, Cayman Islands
**Depth:** 14.3 km
**Tsunami:** Yes — warning issued, later canceled
**Casualties/Damage:** None reported

**Event summary:**
A major M7.6 struck the western Caribbean on February 8, 2025 at 23:23 UTC. The earthquake occurred on the **Swan Islands transform fault**, a left-lateral strike-slip fault along the boundary between the **North American and Caribbean tectonic plates**. At this location, the North American plate moves west-southwest relative to the Caribbean plate at approximately 20 mm/year.

The Pacific Tsunami Warning Center issued warnings for coasts within 1,000 km, covering the Cayman Islands, Jamaica, Cuba, Mexico, Honduras, Belize, Haiti, Costa Rica, Panama, Nicaragua, Guatemala, and the Bahamas. Cuba faced potential waves of 3–10 feet; the Cayman Islands faced 1–3 feet.

By ~02:00 UTC, the PTWC issued an all-clear, with sea level data confirming no hazardous tsunami had materialized. No injuries or significant structural damage were reported. The remote offshore location limited ground-shaking impacts.

**Geological note:** This was the most powerful Caribbean earthquake since a M7.2 struck Haiti in 2021. Ten M6+ events had occurred within 250 km of this location in the preceding century, making it a historically active but infrequently catastrophic zone.

### 2. M7.6 — Drake Passage (2025-10-10)

**Location:** Drake Passage (~60°S, 62°W)
**Depth:** 5.6 km (very shallow)
**Tsunami:** Yes — warning issued for Chile, canceled ~1 hour later
**Casualties/Damage:** None reported

**Event summary:**
A shallow M7.6 struck the Drake Passage on October 10, 2025. The very shallow depth (5.6 km) amplified its tsunami-generating potential. Chile's SHOA marine authority and the PTWC issued tsunami warnings for Chilean coastal areas, lifted approximately one hour later after confirming no significant wave propagation. This was notably the same day as an M7.4 in Mindanao, Philippines — twin major earthquakes on opposite ends of the Pacific.

The Drake Passage straddles the **Scotia plate** boundary zone between the South American and Antarctic plates — a highly seismically active but extremely remote region with no permanent population.

### 3. M7.5 — Southern Drake Passage (2025-08-22)

**Location:** ~710 km SE of Ushuaia, Argentina
**Depth:** 10 km
**Tsunami:** Brief advisory for Antarctic coasts, no threat confirmed
**Casualties/Damage:** None

**Event summary:**
A M7.5 struck at 02:16 UTC on August 22, 2025 in the southern Drake Passage. Chilean authorities issued an advisory for potential tsunami along parts of Antarctica, but the PTWC quickly confirmed no threat to populated coasts. No evacuation orders were issued for Chile or Argentina. A M5.1 aftershock followed within an hour.

### 4. M7.4 — Drake Passage (2025-05-02)

**Location:** ~222 km S of Ushuaia, Argentina
**Depth:** 10 km
**Tsunami:** Yes — evacuation of ~1,800 people; warning canceled
**Casualties/Damage:** None

**Event summary:**
The first major Drake Passage event of 2025 struck May 2. The PTWC warned of waves of 1–3 meters along parts of the Chilean coast. Chile's SENAPRED ordered coastal evacuations in Puerto Williams, Puerto Natales, Porvenir, Río Verde, and Antarctic research bases Prat and O'Higgins — approximately **1,800 people** evacuated. All orders were canceled later in the day with no significant tsunami recorded. More than 50 aftershocks followed, including a M6.4.

### 5. M7.3 — Sand Point, Alaska (2025-07-16)

**Location:** 55 miles S of Sand Point, Alaska Peninsula
**Depth:** 38 km
**Tsunami:** Warning issued, downgraded to advisory within ~1 hour, canceled by ~2:45 PM
**Casualties/Damage:** None; ~3-inch water rise measured at Sand Point buoy

**Event summary:**
A M7.3 struck the Alaska Peninsula at 12:37 PM AKDT on July 16, 2025, felt from Anchorage to Juneau. The National Tsunami Warning Center issued an immediate warning, triggering evacuations of Sand Point, King Cove, Kodiak, Old Harbor, Homer, Seward, and Unalaska. A NOAA buoy measured only a 0.2-foot (3-inch) water rise at Sand Point — no significant waves. The warning was fully canceled by 2:45 PM.

The aftershock sequence was prolific: **40 aftershocks in the first 3 hours**, over **2,700 aftershocks total** in July, with the largest being the M6.2 on July 20 (also in the dataset). The Sand Point area lies on the **Alaska-Aleutian subduction zone**, where the Pacific plate subducts under the North American plate.

## Notable Secondary Event: Venezuela M6.2/M6.3 Sequence (2025-09-24/25)

**Location:** Near Mene Grande, Zulia State, Venezuela
**Depth:** ~9–14 km
**Casualties:** 1 death (heart attack), ~110 injuries/hospitalizations
**Structural damage:** 44+ homes collapsed, additional homes damaged

Though not in the top 5 by magnitude, the Zulia earthquake sequence was the **only Americas event in 2025 with confirmed human casualties and structural damage**. Four strong earthquakes struck the oil-producing Zulia region in rapid succession. The M6.2 and M6.3 events were felt in 64 locations across Venezuela, Colombia, and Aruba, triggering evacuations, power outages, ground fissures, and collapsed homes in Mene Grande and Pampán. A 70-year-old man died of a heart attack attributed to panic.

**Geological context:** The Venezuela earthquakes occurred near the **South Caribbean deformed belt** and the **Boconó fault system**, where the South American and Caribbean plates interact. This region has a long history of destructive seismic events.

## Patterns and Observations

### Drake Passage clustering
Three M7.4+ events struck the Drake Passage in a single year (May, August, October 2025), making it by far the most seismically active subregion for large events in the Americas in 2025. This region sits on the **Scotia arc plate boundary**, where the South American plate, Antarctic plate, and Scotia plate interact. All three events triggered tsunami warnings or advisories; none produced damaging waves, owing to the extreme remoteness (~60°S latitude).

### Tsunami warnings without impact
All four tsunami warnings issued for top-magnitude Americas events in 2025 were canceled within hours, with no significant waves recorded. The highest confirmed wave was a 3-inch rise at Sand Point, Alaska. The pattern suggests: (a) depth and/or rupture geometry limited tsunami generation, and (b) early-warning systems functioned effectively, triggering precautionary evacuations that proved unnecessary but appropriate.

### Human impact concentrated in lower-magnitude events
The only casualties and structural damage in the Americas came from the M6.2/M6.3 Venezuela sequence — not the M7+ events. This reflects the critical role of **proximity to population centers** versus raw magnitude. The M7+ events were all in remote ocean or near-uninhabited coastlines.

### Alaska aftershock productivity
The Sand Point M7.3 generated over 2,700 aftershocks in the following weeks, demonstrating high seismic productivity in the Alaska-Aleutian subduction zone.
