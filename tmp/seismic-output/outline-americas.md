# Americas — Seismic Activity Outline (2025)

---

## 1. Regional Overview

- **Total M6.0+ events:** 39
- **Magnitude range:** M6.0 – M7.6
- **Geographic spread:** Arctic to Antarctic; active zones include Alaska-Aleutian arc, Caribbean plate boundary, Drake Passage/Scotia arc, and northern South America
- **Subregions with major events:**
  - Drake Passage / Southern South America (3 M7.4+ events)
  - Caribbean (Cayman Islands M7.6)
  - Alaska Peninsula (Sand Point M7.3)
  - Northern Venezuela (Zulia M6.2/M6.3 sequence)

## 3. Impact Analysis

### Casualties and Injuries
- **Deaths:** 1 (heart attack attributed to panic, Venezuela M6.2/M6.3)
- **Injuries:** ~110 (Venezuela sequence)
- **All M7+ events:** zero casualties

### Structural Damage
- **Venezuela (Zulia):** 44+ homes collapsed; additional homes damaged; ground fissures; power outages
- **All other events:** No significant structural damage reported

### Economic Damage
- No significant economic losses reported for M7+ events
- Venezuela oil-producing region (Zulia) affected; no quantified production disruption reported

### Infrastructure
- Evacuations for M7.4 Drake Passage (May): ~1,800 people across five Chilean communities and two Antarctic research stations
- Evacuations for M7.3 Alaska: Sand Point, King Cove, Kodiak, Old Harbor, Homer, Seward, Unalaska

## 4. Geological Context

### Plate Boundaries and Fault Systems

- **Alaska-Aleutian Subduction Zone** (Sand Point M7.3): Pacific plate subducts under North American plate; historically prolific aftershock generator
- **North American / Caribbean Transform Boundary** (Cayman Islands M7.6): Swan Islands transform fault; left-lateral strike-slip motion at ~20 mm/year; most powerful Caribbean event since M7.2 Haiti 2021
- **Scotia Arc / Drake Passage** (three M7.4+ events): Interaction of South American, Antarctic, and Scotia plates; shallow crustal seismicity; extremely remote (~60°S)
- **South Caribbean Deformed Belt / Boconó Fault System** (Venezuela M6.2/M6.3): South American–Caribbean plate interaction; historically destructive; near densely populated and oil-producing areas

### Depth Profile
- Drake Passage events: 5.6–10 km (very shallow; high tsunami-generation potential)
- Cayman Islands: 14.3 km (shallow)
- Venezuela: 9–14 km (shallow; near-surface damage potential)
- Alaska: 38 km (intermediate; deeper reduces surface intensity)

---

## 5. Notable Patterns

### Drake Passage Clustering
Three M7.4+ events struck the Drake Passage in a single calendar year (May, August, October). This is an extraordinary concentration of large-magnitude activity in one subregion. All three events triggered tsunami warnings or advisories; none produced damaging waves due to extreme remoteness.

### Tsunami Warning Performance
All four tsunami warnings issued for major Americas events in 2025 were canceled within ~1 hour. Maximum confirmed wave height: 3 inches (Sand Point, Alaska). Pattern indicates:
- Depth and/or rupture geometry limited tsunami generation in each case
- Early-warning systems triggered appropriate precautionary evacuations

### Magnitude vs. Human Impact Inversion
The only event with casualties and structural damage was M6.2/M6.3 Venezuela — well below the top-magnitude threshold. All M7+ events occurred in remote ocean settings. Proximity to population centers and local construction quality dominated impact outcomes over raw magnitude.

### Alaska Aftershock Productivity
Sand Point M7.3 generated 40+ aftershocks in the first 3 hours and 2,700+ total in July — reflecting the high seismic productivity of the Alaska-Aleutian subduction zone. Largest aftershock: M6.2 on July 20.

### Caribbean Seismic Reactivation
The M7.6 Cayman Islands event was the most powerful Caribbean earthquake since Haiti's M7.2 in 2021, occurring on the historically active Swan Islands transform fault.
