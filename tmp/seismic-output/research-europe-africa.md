# Europe-Africa Region: Significant Earthquakes 2025 (M6.0+)

## Overview

The Europe-Africa region recorded 11 M6.0+ earthquakes in 2025 — the fewest of the three regions in this analysis (vs. 94 Asia-Pacific, 39 Americas). Despite the lower count, the region produced some of the most consequential human-impact events of the year, driven by two shallow, populated-area earthquakes in Turkey and the proximity of the Marmara fault system to Istanbul.

## Top Events — Detailed Research

### 1. M6.6 — Central Mid-Atlantic Ridge (March 28, 2025)

**USGS ID:** us7000pnf2
**Coordinates:** 0.756°N, 29.764°W | Depth: 12.3 km
**Tsunami:** No | **PAGER alert:** Green

The largest event in the region was geologically routine but humanly irrelevant: a strike-slip earthquake along a transform fault on the central Mid-Atlantic Ridge, roughly 1,800 km from the nearest coasts (Brazil to the west, West Africa to the east). Only 8 global "Did You Feel It" reports were submitted. No casualties, no damage, no tsunami.

**Geological context:** The Mid-Atlantic Ridge at equatorial latitudes is offset by numerous east-west transform faults accommodating differential spreading between the African/Eurasian plates (east) and South American/North American plates (west). Strike-slip earthquakes on these transforms are common. This event — despite being the region's largest by magnitude — had essentially zero hazard impact.

A nearly simultaneous M6.1 struck the same segment at coordinates 0.706°N, 29.665°W just hours later, likely a foreshock or closely-related aftershock on the same transform system.

### 2. M6.5 — Jan Mayen Island, Svalbard and Jan Mayen (March 10, 2025)

**USGS ID:** us6000pxvx
**Coordinates:** 71.176°N, 8.173°W | Depth: 10 km
**Tsunami:** Yes (precautionary warning issued) | **PAGER alert:** Green

The most geologically unusual event of the year for this region: a shallow M6.5 just 34 km northeast of Olonkinbyen — the only inhabited outpost on Jan Mayen, a remote Norwegian volcanic island in the Arctic with fewer than 20 permanent residents.

**Tsunami:** The USGS tsunami flag was set (indicating NOAA issued a bulletin), and two PTWC/NTWC bulletins were dispatched. However, the NOAA global tsunami database does not record measurable coastal wave heights from this event — the warning was precautionary and quickly downgraded. No coastal impacts were observed or reported anywhere.

**Geological context:** Jan Mayen sits at a complex triple-junction involving the Jan Mayen Ridge (a microcontinent fragment), the Jan Mayen Fracture Zone, and the Kolbeinsey Ridge segment of the Mid-Atlantic spreading system. This is where the Eurasian and North American plates interact in an oblique and tectonically complex geometry. The focal mechanism shows strike-slip with a thrust component (rake ~170°, dip ~82°), consistent with oblique faulting at a transform-ridge junction. This is one of the most significant earthquakes recorded in the Jan Mayen area in recent decades. The Arctic remoteness meant zero human consequences, but the event is scientifically notable.

### 3. M6.2 — Marmara Ereğlisi, Turkey (April 23, 2025)

**USGS ID:** us7000pufs
**Coordinates:** 40.834°N, 28.190°E | Depth: 12.7 km
**Tsunami:** Yes (6 cm wave at Erdek) | **PAGER alert:** Orange

The most consequential event for human impact in the Europe-Africa region in 2025. The epicenter was in the eastern Sea of Marmara, approximately 65–80 km west of central Istanbul, along the locked North Anatolian Fault (NAF) segment that seismologists have long identified as the single greatest seismic risk to any major world city.

**Impact:**
- 1 fatality (cardiac arrest attributed to earthquake panic)
- At least 359 people injured
- Approximately 4,295 buildings damaged across the Marmara region
- Maximum community intensity: MMI VIII
- 934 "Did You Feel It" reports submitted — among the highest felt-report counts of any event in the full 2025 global dataset

**Tsunami:** A small local tsunami occurred. NOAA records a 6 cm wave at Erdek on the southern Marmara coast. No destructive tsunami impact: the Marmara Sea's enclosed geometry limits far-field wave propagation.

**Geological context:** The Sea of Marmara sits directly above the westward continuation of the North Anatolian Fault — a major right-lateral strike-slip system comparable to California's San Andreas. The 1999 Izmit M7.6 and Düzce M7.2 earthquakes ruptured the NAF east of the Marmara. Stress transfer from those ruptures has loaded the Marmara "Istanbul segment," which has not produced a major earthquake since 1766. Seismologists estimate a 35–70% probability of a M7+ earthquake on this segment within the coming decades. This M6.2 is a moderate-sized event on or near that system — significant in terms of impact, but well short of the scenario researchers most fear. The strike-slip focal mechanism (scalar moment 2.89 × 10¹⁸ N·m) is consistent with NAF kinematics.

### 4. M6.2 — Karpathos Sea, Greece (May 22, 2025)

**USGS ID:** us7000q0pq
**Coordinates:** 35.892°N, 25.916°E | Depth: 64 km
**Tsunami:** Yes (5 cm at Heraklion) | **PAGER alert:** Yellow

A moderate intermediate-depth earthquake in the Karpathos/eastern Crete sector of the Hellenic subduction zone. The 64 km depth attenuated near-surface shaking significantly, but the event was still widely felt across Crete, Rhodes, and the Dodecanese islands.

**Impact:**
- No fatalities
- Buildings damaged in the Heraklion (Irakleio) area of Crete
- Landslides near Marathitis
- 819 "Did You Feel It" reports submitted

**Tsunami:** A 5 cm wave was recorded at Heraklion. The USGS event record explicitly notes this wave. No tsunami warning was issued (the automated system threshold was not triggered by the wave height), and there were no coastal impact reports.

**Geological context:** This region lies above the Hellenic Arc, the most seismically active zone in Europe, where the African plate subducts northward beneath the Aegean microplate at roughly 35 mm/year. The 64 km depth is consistent with intraslab seismicity within the subducting African lithosphere — the same tectonic environment that produced the catastrophic 1956 Amorgos earthquake (M7.7) and the 2021 Crete M6.3. The intermediate focal depth, while reducing local intensity, allows shaking to spread over a wide area, explaining the broad distribution of felt reports across the central and eastern Mediterranean.

### 5. M6.1 — Bigadiç, Turkey (August 10, 2025)

**USGS ID:** us6000qznb
**Coordinates:** 39.371°N, 28.017°E | Depth: 10 km
**Tsunami:** No | **PAGER alert:** Orange

A shallow earthquake in Balıkesir Province, western Turkey — the most damaging event relative to its magnitude in the Europe-Africa dataset. The 10 km depth produced violent near-field shaking: maximum MMI reached 9.0 (Violent), with community intensity reports averaging 7.9 from 237 submissions.

**Impact:**
- 1 person killed
- 52 people injured
- At least 6 buildings destroyed in Balıkesir Province
- 863 buildings damaged in Balıkesir Province
- At least 8 homes and 105 buildings damaged in Manisa Province
- A sinkhole reported in the Odunpazarı area
- Ground failure: Yellow alert for landslide hazard (~200 people exposed)

**Geological context:** Western Turkey (the Aegean extensional province) is among the most seismically active continental regions in Europe, dominated by E-W-trending normal faults as the Aegean lithosphere stretches north-south in the back-arc behind the Hellenic subduction zone. Bigadiç lies in the Balıkesir region of this extensional system, where numerous NE-SW and E-W normal fault systems have produced destructive historical earthquakes. The combination of shallow depth (10 km), brittle upper-crust normal faulting, and proximity to population centers made this M6.1 disproportionately destructive relative to its magnitude.

## Regional Patterns and Observations

### Turkey: Highest Human Impact

Turkey produced 3 of the 11 Europe-Africa events (M6.2 Marmara Ereğlisi in April, M6.1 Bigadiç in August, M6.0 Sındırgı in October) and accounted for all fatalities and the vast majority of injuries and building damage. Two distinct tectonic regimes are represented:
- **North Anatolian Fault system** (Marmara): right-lateral strike-slip, the "ticking clock" beneath Istanbul
- **Aegean extensional province** (Bigadiç, Sındırgı): normal faulting driven by back-arc spreading

The October M6.0 near Sındırgı is notable for its 8 km depth — even shallower than Bigadiç — though PAGER details were not available.

### Mediterranean Arc: Deep Events, Wide Felt Area

Greece's two events (M6.2 Karpathos and M6.0 Fry/Dodecanese) were both intermediate-depth (64–76 km) intraslab events within the subducting African plate beneath the Hellenic Arc. Deep subduction earthquakes in this region are capable of being felt across the entire eastern Mediterranean basin. Both events generated measurable but non-destructive tsunami waves, consistent with the complex bathymetry of the Aegean and eastern Mediterranean.

### Ocean Ridge Events: Seismically Active, Humanly Irrelevant

Five of the 11 events occurred in remote ocean settings (Mid-Atlantic Ridge, South Sandwich Islands, southwest of Africa), accounting for the region's largest magnitude (M6.6) but zero human impact. These events are scientifically routine and represent normal plate boundary tectonics with no hazard consequence.

### Tsunami Activity

Three events in this region generated small tsunamis:
- **Jan Mayen M6.5**: Precautionary bulletin issued; no recorded wave heights
- **Marmara Ereğlisi M6.2**: 6 cm at Erdek — enclosed Marmara Sea limited propagation
- **Karpathos M6.2**: 5 cm at Heraklion — Hellenic Arc subduction geometry

None reached destructive heights. The absence of a large subduction-zone thrust earthquake in the region in 2025 (comparable to the Hellenic Arc's historical tsunamigenic events) kept tsunami risk in check.
