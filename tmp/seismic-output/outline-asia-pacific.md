# Global Seismic Activity Report 2025

---

## 1. Regional Overview

- **Event count:** 94 of 144 global M6.0+ events (65% of global total)
- **Magnitude range:** M6.0–M8.8
- **Geographic spread:** Russia (Kamchatka/Kuril Islands), Japan (Sanriku coast), Myanmar (central/Sagaing region), Philippines (Mindanao)
- **Tectonic settings:** Subduction zones (4 of 5 top events), continental strike-slip (1 of 5)
- **Significance:** Region contained 4 of the 5 largest earthquakes globally in 2025, including the strongest worldwide since 2011

## 3. Impact Analysis

### Casualties
- **Total deaths (top 5 events):** ~3,607–5,359
- **Deadliest event:** M7.7 Myanmar — ~3,600–5,352 killed, >11,000 injured, ~17 million people affected
- **Secondary deaths:** 103 killed in Thailand (Bangkok building collapses from Myanmar earthquake)
- **Near-zero fatalities:** M8.8 Kamchatka despite record magnitude — 1 indirect death, 21 injuries during evacuation

### Economic and Infrastructure Damage
- **Myanmar:** >157,000 buildings damaged/destroyed; 3 hospitals destroyed; Ava Bridge (1934) collapsed; ~90% of Sagaing City homes destroyed
- **Japan (Aomori):** ~4,000 buildings damaged; critical infrastructure intact; nuclear facilities unaffected
- **Philippines:** Hospital and school buildings damaged; power outages in coastal areas
- **Kamchatka:** Coastal towns sustained severe wave damage; remote location limited broader economic impact

### Evacuations
- 1.9 million people evacuated in Japan following M8.8 Kamchatka tsunami warning
- 90,000+ evacuated in northeastern Japan following M7.6 Aomori event

## 4. Geological Context

### Ring of Fire Dominance
- Four of five top events occurred on Ring of Fire subduction structures
- Kuril–Kamchatka Trench: Pacific Plate subducts beneath Okhotsk microplate at 79–85 mm/yr; history of megathrust events (1737 ~M9.0–9.3; 1952 M8.8–9.0)
- Japan Trench (Sanriku): Pacific Plate subducts beneath Okhotsk Plate; same zone as 2011 Tōhoku M9.0
- Philippine Trench/Mindanao: Philippine Sea Plate subduction; one of the world's most seismically complex plate boundaries

### Continental Fault Exception
- Myanmar M7.7 ruptured the Sagaing Fault — a ~1,400 km right-lateral strike-slip transform boundary between the Burma Microplate and Sunda Plate
- Not a subduction event; the most lethal earthquake of the year originated from a continental intraplate structure, not the Ring of Fire

## 5. Notable Patterns

### Kamchatka Sequence Clustering
- Single subduction zone generated ~4 of the region's 5 largest events (foreshock + mainshock + 2 major aftershocks)
- Foreshock: M7.4 on July 20 preceded M8.8 by 9 days
- Main sequence: 2,877+ aftershocks recorded; active through at least October
- September aftershocks (M7.4 Sep 13, M7.8 Sep 18) occurred 45–51 days post-mainshock — illustrating extended seismic hazard windows following megathrust events

### Supershear Rupture (Myanmar)
- Myanmar M7.7 classified as a supershear earthquake — rupture velocity exceeded local shear wave speed
- 530 km fault section ruptured, including a previously identified seismic gap accumulating stress since 1839
- Rare rupture mode concentrates destructive energy forward along strike; amplified damage in Mandalay and surrounding areas

### Tsunami Underperformance
- For 4 of 5 events with tsunami warnings, observed wave heights were significantly lower than forecast maximums
- M8.8 Kamchatka tsunami notably modest relative to event magnitude — attracted scientific study of source heterogeneity and rupture geometry
- Zero tsunami-direct fatalities across all five events

### Human Impact Asymmetry
- M8.8 Kamchatka (largest energy release): ~1 death — remote location, effective warnings, rapid evacuation
- M7.7 Myanmar (third largest by magnitude): up to 5,352 deaths — shallow depth (10 km), dense urban setting, inadequate building stock, supershear rupture mode
- Pattern underscores that magnitude is a poor standalone predictor of casualties; depth, urbanization, and infrastructure quality are dominant factors
